Sheet 02 / 07Product

Meet HALO, the front desk robot.

HALO sits at reception and takes the questions a front desk hears a hundred times a day. It greets visitors as they approach, answers from your building's own knowledge, lets the host know, and hands over to a person whenever it should. Your team keeps the visitors who need them.

01Perceives

It tracks people in the lobby as anonymous shapes from the moment they approach, and it knows whether someone is speaking to it or to the person beside them. No faces are recognised or stored.

02Understands

English, Hindi, Marathi, Tamil, Japanese and Czech. It follows interruptions, and it follows a language switched mid sentence. Names, times and intent.

03Acts

It checks the visitor in, messages the host on Teams, Slack, WhatsApp or email, alerts the right team, issues a guest pass and logs the visit.

04Knows its limits

When it cannot help, it says so and brings in a person, with a summary of the conversation so far.

Questions we get

It does not move. Why?

By design. At a front desk the value is in perception, conversation and action. A stationary unit has no fall risk, runs all day and costs less. Mobility arrives further up the ladder, on the same stack.

Is this a voice assistant in a shell?

A voice assistant answers questions. HALO perceives who is in front of it, knows when it is being addressed, answers from your site's own knowledge and acts inside your systems, such as messaging a host or checking a visitor in.

A camera at reception. What about privacy?

HALO is face-free by default. It detects that a person is present without identifying who. Anything personal needs consent, processing happens on the device wherever possible, and visitor data stays with you, in India.

Will it replace the receptionist?

No. HALO takes the repetitive questions so the front desk team can look after the visitors who need a person. Every deployment includes a human handoff.

Sheet 03 / 07Approach

Physics sits between perception and action.

Most robot learning today maps what a machine sees and hears directly to movement. The model is trained on demonstrations of particular tasks, so it is skilled at those tasks and brittle at their edges. A new object, a new surface or a new grip is outside the data, and the behaviour falls apart.

Unskild adds the missing layer. Our Vision-Language-Physics-Action architecture, VL-Physics-A, places physical reasoning between perception and action, so a robot's behaviour is grounded in how objects, forces and contact actually behave. A robot that understands why a cup slides can hold a glass it has never seen.

Sheet 06 / 07Company

The name comes from shoshin.

Shoshin is the beginner's mind of Zen practice. A skilled robot has been trained on particular tasks and is lost outside them. An unskilled one starts from an understanding of the world and learns each task from first principles. We named the company after the second kind.

In the wordmark, the N and the I stand taller than the other letters. Read together, they are the AI inside Unskild.

Founder Sachin Panicker

A linguist by training, with research in multimodal pragmatics across Japanese, English and Czech, presented at Charles University in Prague and at the United Nations. Two decades in enterprise AI, now as Chief AI Officer of a global technology firm, where he leads a thirty-strong team building an enterprise agentic AI platform.
